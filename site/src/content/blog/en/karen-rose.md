There is a particular moment in Karen-Rose's soap workshop when the loaf comes
out of its wooden mould. It has been sitting there since the session before,
hardening, and it is cut into bars with a wire — dried flowers running all the
way through the block, so every bar has a different face.

That is the shape of everything she teaches. You do not decorate something
half-finished; you start with the raw ingredients and leave with the object.

## Seven formats, one bench

Karen-Rose runs seven different workshops out of her studio in Treptow, and
they divide into three families.

**Natural cosmetics.** The three-hour *Bio Naturkosmetik Workshop* (€65) is the
full version — melting, weighing, essential oils by the drop, and the finished
bar in your hands. The two-hour *Essentials* cut (€49) is the same bench in
less time, for an evening rather than an afternoon. The *Shampoo* workshop (€55,
three hours) is its own thing: a solid bar, worked to a paste and pressed into
the mould.

**Soap and candles.** *Seife Sieden* (€99, three hours) is the one above — real
cold-process soap, which is why it is the most expensive and the one that sends
you home empty-handed and calls you back later. The *Duftkerzen* workshop (€55,
two hours) is vegan and made to last: scent, wax, and a jar you will refill.

**Terrazzo.** Two classes, both €59 and three hours. *Terrazzo Schmuck* is
jewellery — chips crushed, set, ground back and polished. *Keramikgießen* is
the same material poured as ceramics.

## Who it suits

Groups of four to twelve, which is small enough that she is at your shoulder
when the mixture does something unexpected. Sessions run in German. Two to
three hours depending on the format, so it fits an evening.

It works for a team that has run out of ideas for the offsite, and it works for
one person on a Saturday who wants to know what is actually in the bottle in
their bathroom. Both end the same way: you take it with you.
