Picture your team on a Tuesday afternoon. Laptops closed. Sleeves rolled up.
Your quietest developer is laughing because her bowl has collapsed for the
second time, and the head of sales is asking her how she got the walls so thin
on the first try.

Nobody is talking about Q4. Everybody is talking to each other.

That is what a good team building event looks like, and it is surprisingly
easy to book.

## Why most team events don't build much

The usual options all have a catch. A bar night is fun for the people who
drink and talk loudly, and quietly skipped by everyone else. An escape room
rewards the two fastest thinkers while the rest watch. A dinner puts people
next to the colleagues they already sit next to every day.

None of them is bad. They just don't do much for team cohesion, because they
let everyone stay in the role they already have.

## What a workshop does differently

**Everyone starts at zero.** In a pottery, mosaic or screen printing workshop,
the CEO is as much a beginner as the intern. Being equally new at something,
and a bit clumsy at it together, is one of the fastest ways to make people
relax around each other.

**Busy hands make talking easy.** When your hands are in clay, conversation
happens on its own. People who never speak up in meetings chat across the
table, ask for help, pass the tools. No icebreaker games needed.

**It includes everyone.** No alcohol required, no fitness level, no language
barrier. Most of our hosts teach in English and German, so international teams
and new hires join in from the first minute.

**Everyone takes something home.** A mug, a candle, a printed tote bag, a
small mosaic. It sits on a desk for months and every time someone looks at it,
they remember the afternoon. That is a lot more than a group photo nobody
opens again.

**It feels like a thank you.** A workshop with a real Berlin maker, in their
studio, says "we thought about this" in a way a standard team event rarely
does. Your team notices.

## What it looks like in practice

A typical team workshop runs two to three hours. Long enough to make
something real, short enough to fit into an afternoon or the start of an
evening. Some ideas from our Berlin hosts:

- **Ceramics** on the wheel or by hand, at a pottery studio in Kreuzberg or
  Neukölln
- **Candle making** with dried flowers and your own scent
- **Mosaic**, where everyone makes their own piece (also on-site at your
  office or event location)
- **Screen printing** your own tote bags or shirts, with a mobile print studio
  that comes to you
- **Terrariums**, a calm one for big groups, on-site for up to 150 people
- **Sushi or kimchi** for teams that prefer to eat what they make

Most formats work from about 6 people, many up to 30 in the studio, and some
come to your office for much bigger groups. Prices usually start around €35 to
€50 per person, with all materials included.

## How to book it without a planning headache

You don't need to call ten studios. Tell us:

1. how many people you are
2. roughly when (a date, or "a weekday afternoon in November")
3. where: at the maker's studio or at your place
4. anything we should know, like budget, languages or dietary needs for food
   workshops

We match you with the right maker, check availability and send you a proposal.
You confirm directly with the host. For bigger events we can also combine
several hosts, so people can choose between, say, ceramics and candle making
in the same evening.
