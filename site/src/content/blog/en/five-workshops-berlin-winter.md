Berlin winters are long, and there is only so much museum a person can take. The
good news: most of the city's makers work indoors, and a workshop is a far better
way to spend a grey Saturday than another cafe.

## Start with clay

Wheel throwing looks harder than it is. You will produce something lopsided in
the first hour and something recognisable in the second, and either way you go
home with a piece you made. **Helka** runs both wheel throwing and handbuilding,
so bigger groups are not a problem.

## Then something with light

Cyanotype printing turns sunlight into deep Prussian-blue prints, and it works
even on a flat winter day — it just takes longer. **Sabine Alex** brings a mobile
darkroom, so this one can happen in your own space.

## And something you can drink

**Angelo** roasts coffee East-African style, in a pot over an open flame. You
grind what you roast and drink it there, which is a very good way to end an
afternoon.

> The point of a workshop is not the object you take home. It is the two hours
> you spent not looking at a screen.

## The practical bit

Most sessions run two to four hours and take between four and twenty people.
Nearly all of them are beginner-friendly, and several of the makers will travel
to your office if you would rather not move a whole team across the city.
