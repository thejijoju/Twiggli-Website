We have booked a lot of team sessions by now, and the difference between one
that lands and one that politely fills two hours is fairly predictable.

## Everyone has to make something

The sessions that fall flat are the ones where people watch. If half the team is
holding a phone, the workshop has already failed. Every format we list is
hands-on, and everyone leaves with the thing they made.

## Nobody should be good at it

A workshop where two people are already skilled and everyone else is lost is a
demonstration, not an activity. Beginner-friendly is not a compromise — it is the
whole point. Being equally bad at something together is what does the work.

## It should be hard to talk shop

Something about having your hands in clay makes it difficult to keep discussing
the roadmap. That is a feature. The conversations that happen over a shared table
are the reason to do this at all.

## Two hours, not a day

Long sessions drift. Most of our formats run two to four hours, which is enough
to make something real and not so long that people start checking the time.
