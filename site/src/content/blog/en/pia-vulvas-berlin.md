Pia's room is full of paintings. That is where this started: friends would come
round, look at the walls and say they would love to paint too, they just never
know how to begin. So they asked her to paint with them.

A blank canvas is a surprisingly hard thing to face, and Pia noticed that it
was not the painting itself her friends struggled with. It was the first step.
Sitting with them, she found out something about herself: she loves painting
*with* people. Figuring out what someone wants to make, and then helping them
bring it to life.

## Five years in women's health

Before the workshops, Pia spent more than five years at Clue, the Berlin period
tracking app used by around ten million people every month, as a product
manager and later a director. She interviewed women about the most intimate
topics there are, and she learned a lot. More than she expected.

The lesson that stayed with her: there is so much to do in women's health, and
most of us do not even know what we do not know about our own bodies. She
wanted to do something with everything she had learned. For a while she
thought about a menstrual cycle workshop.

## Painting as a container

Then the two things came together. Painting would be the container, the
conversations would happen inside it.

Why vulvas? Because the vulva is the visible part, the tip of the iceberg, of
so many menstrual, reproductive and sexual topics that have been kept quiet for
centuries. If we can talk about this, we can start talking about everything
else.

## What actually happens

The workshop is two things at once. It is about building a language: anatomy,
the vagina, the clitoris, the words most of us never learned at school. And it
is simply art, a beautiful way to spend an evening together and make something
with your hands.

It is educational, not sexual. There is no nudity and no live model. Men and
people coming on their own are welcome, and nobody needs to have painted
before.

People leave with three things: a painting for their wall, new knowledge about
their bodies, and often the urge to keep the conversation going. Pia's hope is
that the paintings live in people's homes and keep sparking those talks long
after the evening.

## Never the same evening twice

The paint and the brushes stay the same, but the conversation at the end of
each workshop depends entirely on who came. New topics come up every time.

> "It's always interesting. I really, really enjoy facilitating this and being
> with people through this experience." — Pia
